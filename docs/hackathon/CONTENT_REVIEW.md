# Sample content review

This records a bounded independent comparison of the real hosted sample and an edited copy with the project's original [garden chapter](../../backend/sample/chapter.txt). It is not owner acceptance, a subject-matter certification or a guarantee for arbitrary documents. No additional provider request was made for this review.

The TXT, PDF and DOCX samples were extracted with the existing local extraction code. After whitespace normalization, all three matched the original TXT text; they produced six, one and eleven passages respectively. Their different passage boundaries do not change the source text. The [sample notice](../../backend/sample/LICENSE.txt) and project MIT license govern these original project files.

## Original hosted result

The funded hosted job produced ten sections, three quiz questions and 31 attached quotations. All quotations resolved exactly to the original sample. The introduction had no quotations and was correctly marked for review. Further inspection found problems that were not all caught by the automated support review:

| Section | Observed support problem |
| --- | --- |
| Objectives | Broader effective-irrigation and actual-plant-needs language was not supported by attached evidence |
| Introduction | Uncited garden-health, evaporation, under/overwatering and root-development claims exceeded the source |
| Readings | “Significantly” strengthened the source's conditional language; the fixed-schedules conclusion exceeded this section's quoted evidence |
| Notes | Daily-changing conditions and decision guidance were outside its own quotations; a general exact-depth instruction dropped the source's approximation |
| Announcement | Efficient-resource-use and appropriate-care assurances were absent from the sample |
| Rubric | Proposed scoring was mixed with an uncited requirement to establish actual environmental causes |
| First quiz | The question and answer dropped “about,” although its explanation and evidence preserved the approximation |

These are support failures within the supplied material, not findings that the broader claims are true or false in the outside world. Exact quotations, valid structure and successful model calls do not establish complete entailment.

## Edited hosted copy

Seven text-only corrections were saved through the real application editors to a second, cached course, advancing its version from 5 to 12. The original first course and raw exports remained unchanged. The edits made no provider calls, changed no citations and did not erase the existing review or dependency flags.

The corrected objectives describe proposed learning tasks. The introduction now states that it lacks adequate cited evidence rather than presenting an uncited factual introduction. Readings preserve the source's “about,” “may,” “can” and “begins.” Notes stay within their own record-keeping quotations. The announcement stays within the quoted two-container scenario. The rubric identifies its assessment design as proposed criteria and discusses possible influences and observation limits. The first quiz preserves “about” in both question and answer.

The unchanged assignment is explicitly labeled a proposed task. Its three-day, morning/evening log is study design; its factual premises preserve “approximately” and “may” and have attached source quotations. The second quiz's slow-watering answer and explanation match its quotations. The third quiz uses the source's exact two-container scenario and its stated response.

A second bounded comparison found no further concrete unsupported factual addition in that edited copy. **All ten sections remain `needs_review`**: seven edited sections plus three assessments whose dependencies changed. The course remains `needs_review`. The introduction remains incomplete as teaching content because it deliberately contains an evidence-review notice. No automatic support recheck or owner acceptance is implied.

## Demonstration and use

The edited copy can demonstrate inspection, correction and version history with the remaining warnings visible. Identify a cached course as cached and a previously generated result as previously generated. Do not describe the edited copy as a fresh model result or remove review flags merely to make the demonstration look finished.

Before using the course as teaching material, compare every factual claim, quiz answer and explanation with that section's cited quotations. Preserve approximations and uncertainty, distinguish proposed activities from source-established requirements, and keep unsupported material flagged or remove it. Adding a plausible statement from another passage is not the same as attaching adequate evidence to this section.
